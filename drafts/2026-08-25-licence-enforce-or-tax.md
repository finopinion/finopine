Put four regulators in a room with the same problem and they will pick different tools. Australia reached for a licence. The United States reached for enforcement. India reached for the tax code. The United Kingdom reached for the promotions regime. Each was defensible on its own terms. The outcomes diverged more than the intentions did.

The usual way to compare these is on a strictness axis, from permissive to hostile. That axis explains almost nothing. A licensing regime with a high bar is stricter in effect than an enforcement campaign that produces a handful of cases, and yet the licensing country keeps its industry while the enforcement country exports it.

## Instruments have shapes

A **licence** is prospective and addressed to firms. It says: here is the bar, clear it by this date, and you may operate. The cost is high but it is knowable, and knowability is what lets a business raise capital and hire compliance staff. Australia folded platforms into its existing financial services licence rather than building something bespoke, which means the obligations arrived pre-argued.

**Enforcement** is retrospective and addressed to conduct. It says: we will tell you afterwards. For a well-capitalised incumbent that is survivable. For a firm deciding where to incorporate, it is close to unpriceable, and unpriceable risk does not attract capital. The American approach produced sophisticated legal argument and a steady migration of registration offshore.

**Tax** is neither. It does not test conduct or capability. India's flat rate on virtual digital asset gains, coupled with a deduction at source on transactions, was not aimed at whether a platform held customer assets safely. It was aimed at visibility and revenue. The predictable effect was a collapse in onshore volume, not a change in behaviour.

**Promotions rules** sit somewhere else again. Britain regulated the invitation rather than the activity, which reaches marketing and leaves market structure largely alone.

> The question a regulator is actually answering is not "how much of this do we want" but "which surface do we touch". Firms, conduct, revenue, or speech.

## What follows from the shape

Licensing consolidates. It favours firms that can carry fixed compliance cost, which means fewer and larger domestic operators, and it disadvantages small local entrants relative to large foreign ones with licences elsewhere. That is a real cost and it is rarely acknowledged by advocates.

Enforcement disperses. Firms that can leave, leave. What remains is the part of the industry that cannot relocate, which is not the same as the part you would have chosen to keep.

Tax relocates the transaction rather than the firm. Volume moves to venues outside the reporting perimeter, which is the opposite of the visibility the measure was designed to produce.

Promotions rules change the marketing and leave the rest, which is either an efficient minimal intervention or a cosmetic one depending on what you thought the problem was.

## The counter-case, and it is strong

Two objections deserve answering.

The first is sequencing. The United States did not choose enforcement over licensing so much as arrive at enforcement because legislating was blocked. Reading that as a deliberate instrument choice flatters the process. A regulator with existing statutory tools and no new mandate will use the tools it has.

The second is that market size swamps all of this. The American market is deep enough to absorb regulatory friction that would empty a smaller one, and the Indian market is growing fast enough that a tax shock may prove a delay rather than a redirection. If that is right, the instrument was noise and scale was signal.

## What would settle it

Domestic licensed platform counts and onshore volume share, tracked over three years across the four. If the licensing jurisdictions hold their industry while the enforcement and tax jurisdictions continue exporting theirs, the instrument mattered. If the four converge regardless, it did not, and the honest conclusion is that regulators have less influence over where this activity sits than any of them believe.
